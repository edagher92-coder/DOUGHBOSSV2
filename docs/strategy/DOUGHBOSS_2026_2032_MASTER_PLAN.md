## The product architecture and pricing research

### Three corporate product tiers — prices to be calculated, not invented

- **Everyday Office:** minis/bites platter, simple per-person menu, minimum quantity, timed weekday delivery. Designed for office managers, EAs and team lunches. Price from recipe and packaging cost plus kitchen labour, transport, merchant fees, waste, marketing CAC and target contribution.
- **Mediterranean Premium:** signature flavours, dietary alternatives, premium presentation, dessert add-ons. Designed for executive meetings, agencies, events and celebrations.
- **DoughBoss x Snowflow Experience:** savoury platters plus slushies, soft serve, popcorn or fairy floss. Offer staffed and drop-off formats separately, with power, space, setup, staffing, public liability, food-allergen and pack-down requirements priced. Avoid discounting a high-labour event package to the same margin as food drop-off.

Build a **package economics calculator** for each tier: revenue ex GST – ingredients – disposables – production labour – allocated kitchen overhead – delivery – service/event labour – equipment depreciation/cleaning – merchant fees – discount/refund allowance = contribution. Review each contract before recurring discounts. Establish minimum order, lead time, delivery radius, cancellation and late-change policy.

### Market assessment

Corporate pizza catering is an existing market, not an unserved category. Made in Italy already markets Sydney CBD office pizza catering; aggregator EatFirst lists multiple Sydney and Melbourne caterers. DoughBoss differentiation should be Mediterranean minis, reliable delivery windows, coherent savoury/sweet experience, self-service ordering, consolidated billing and account management. Do not advertise 'first', 'best' or 'cheapest' without substantiation.

### Research before scaling advertisements

- Mystery-shop 10 competitors in Sydney CBD and 10 in Melbourne for price per head, minimums, lead times, cancellation, dietaries, fulfilment, presentation and conversion experience.
- Research real address-based catchments for each site: office density, coworking, hospitals, universities, venues, weekday vs weekend demand, daytime delivery time, loading and parking, competition.
- Interview 20–30 actual buyers (office managers, executive assistants, event coordinators, pub operators); record pain points and willingness to reorder.
- Build a 200-account qualified B2B list. Use consent-compliant contact methods; publicly listed emails are not blanket marketing consent. Record source, contact permission and unsubscribe.

## Central kitchen and field operations

**Current asset:** four cooks in operating Revesby kitchen. Measure units/hour by SKU, available cold storage, shift capacity, ingredient lead time, morning dispatch cutoff, wastage and handoff time; do not treat current production as infinitely scalable. Run a 2x peak-order test plus refrigeration, vehicle failure and product recall exercises.

**SOP set:** approved ingredient and allergen matrix; version-controlled recipe weights and yields; supplier intake; batch/lot number; manufacturing date and use-by validation; cooking/cooling/temperature logs; label; pick/pack; chain of custody; receipt at store; reheating/serving; rejects; returns; complaints; recalls; cleaning and staff training. Use calibrated probes and documented exceptions.

**Regulation gate:** NSW Food Authority states production distributed to other businesses for on-sale can fall under manufacturing/wholesaling requirements. Confirm central kitchen classification/licensing and site-specific obligations with regulator/council. Potentially hazardous food ordinarily travels at <=5°C cold or >=60°C hot unless a validated alternative applies. Melbourne sites need Victorian council registration and a production/supply arrangement; food safety supervisors, allergen and staff rules are location-specific.

**Labour:** fully cost each store using actual rosters, awards, leave, superannuation, penalties, cover and cleaning. The one-to-two-person store concept is a hypothesis to stress-test at peak demand, not a labour budget assumption. New area manager owns daily scorecard; Charlie owns kitchen and store performance.

**Supply chain:** dual-source critical ingredients; forecast daily based on paid orders and expected demand; weekly stock count; transfer pricing between the kitchen, Snowflow and each store; refrigerated distribution route cost/drop; delivery and food-safety exception log.

## Financial projections: scenarios, not forecasts

No actual sales, gross margins, contract values or paid-acquisition data were supplied. The ranges below are **illustrative planning sensitivities**, not probability-weighted predictions, evidence of achieved growth or a promise of DoughBoss results. They assume the same set of operating stores so acquisition-driven growth is excluded, otherwise the comparison is misleading.

| Scenario, relative to baseline at existing equivalent stores | 12-month revenue | 24-month revenue | 12-month operating-profit change | 24-month operating-profit change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Downside: slower conversion, higher food/freight/labour | -10% to +5% | -5% to +10% | -40% to 0% | -25% to +10% |
| Base: successful niche test, repeat B2B, steady margins | +10% to +25% | +20% to +45% | +10% to +35% | +20% to +65% |
| Upside: strong contracts, efficient production, sustained repeat | +25% to +45% | +45% to +80% | +35% to +80% | +65% to +140% |

Operating-profit percentages can be volatile or meaningless where baseline profit is near zero or negative; use absolute A$ contribution and cash as primary measures. **No change** comparator: existing sales mix and costs, no dedicated catering sales engine, no new integrated CRM/batch/route systems; market, price and wage conditions can still alter results. **With changes** comparator: same-store channels and systems improve; new acquisitions require separate capital, opening and ramp models. No causal uplift is guaranteed: test changes through holdouts/cohorts and track incremental contribution, not revenue alone.

**Illustration:** if a site earns A$20,000 monthly sales with 15% contribution (A$3,000), a 20% sales uplift yields A$24,000 sales; at 15% contribution, profit is A$3,600 (+20%). But if added delivery and staffing cut contribution to 10%, profit is A$2,400 (-20%) despite higher sales. This is why marketing-driven revenue growth must not be equated with profit growth.

**Group acquisition math:** 4 stores × A$150k–200k = A$600k–800k purchase prices before refurbishment, fitout, stock, bonds and working capital; five = A$750k–1m. Founder cash A$150k is not the complete financing. Consolidate investor commitment schedules, source/use, covenants, dilution, return terms and minimum cash buffer. Existing equity alone is not spendable cash.

## A-to-Z production line: standard operating design

1. **Demand capture:** website/Square and approved catering quotes enter unified daily forecast by store, delivery slot and SKU; freeze same-day cut-off and flag rush orders.
2. **Recipe/BOM planning:** translate orders into flour, dough, meat, cheese, vegetables, toppings, packaging, slush mix and desserts, including approved yield/waste factors and allergens.
3. **Supplier ordering:** compare stock and reorder points; order against authorised purchase orders; two approved alternatives for critical inputs.
4. **Receiving:** match delivered quantities to PO/invoice, inspect quality/expiry/temperature, log lot numbers and reject exceptions.
5. **Storage:** segregate raw/ready-to-eat, allergen and chilled stock; FIFO/FEFO; calibrated temperature logging.
6. **Pre-production:** clean/sanitise lines, check staff hygiene, equipment settings, recipes, label stock and shift allocation.
7. **Dough and bread:** record mixer batch, hydration, proof time, weights and outputs; measure flour-to-finished yield and per-hour productivity.
8. **Meat and fillings:** butcher-sourced inputs are checked like third-party ingredients; record true landed raw cost, cooking losses, preparation time and safe handling.
9. **Minis/bites assembly:** portion by weight, approved recipe card, allergen controls, in-process rejection and labour minutes/batch.
10. **Cooking/cooling:** validated cook and cooling procedures, logs, separation and shelf-life limits; product-specific food safety plan.
11. **Pack/label:** SKU, amount, ingredients/allergen declarations as applicable, lot and date, destination, storage/reheating directions and traceability.
12. **Cold holding:** monitor capacity and temperatures; batch stock rotation and waste disposition.
13. **Pick/dispatch:** scan batch and store order, reconcile count and allergens, route manifests, scheduled driver and vehicle readiness.
14. **Transport:** hygienic fit-for-purpose vehicle, temperature/time controls, electronic delivery evidence and exception escalation.
15. **Hub receipt:** count, temperature, integrity, lot, discrepancy, storage and shelf-life checks; do not silently book damaged stock as received.
16. **Final fulfilment:** timed customer order picking, presentation/QC, packaging, dispatch, proof of delivery, complaints and return workflow.
17. **Finance loop:** revenue/fees/refunds to Square and Xero, costs allocated to each SKU/channel/store, supplier invoice approval, weekly contribution review.
18. **Continuous improvement:** daily five-minute exception huddle, weekly waste and productivity review, monthly recipe/pricing and supplier review.

## Overhead elimination program — supplier, labour, productivity and assets

**Existing supply advantage (owner reported):** an affiliated butcher provides meats at cost, bread and dough are produced in-house, Snowflow-related products and machinery are available at cost, and acquired shops have most equipment. Elie estimates setup savings of A$400k–500k per store versus a fresh fitout. Treat this as a **replacement-cost avoidance estimate**, not immediate cash profit. Four stores could therefore avoid an illustrative A$1.6m–2m in fresh-build spending, if the counterfactual is valid; avoid presenting it as a verified valuation gain or resale value.

**True economic cost:** affiliated or 'cost-price' goods still consume raw materials, staff time, power, premises, maintenance, depreciation, freight, compliance and working capital. Record each supplier's invoice cost and an internal fully-loaded transfer cost, and let the accountant determine GST and related-party treatments. Hidden subsidies can make a store appear profitable while draining Snowflow or the butcher.

| Lever | Action | With implementation | Without implementation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meat | Monthly yield and market-benchmark cost audit; lock portion weights | Margin visible; wastage controlled | Understated cost and inconsistent portions |
| Dough/minis | Recipe weights, batch yield, proof schedule, production cells | Better throughput, fewer defects | Labour and ingredient waste grows with volume |
| Suppliers | ABC spend list, 3 competitive quotes for non-affiliated categories, order consolidation, rebate transparency | Lower landed unit cost and fewer emergency buys | Fragmented purchasing and price creep |
| Packaging | Standardise sizes and cases; test insulation/quality and branding | Scale buying, lower picking complexity | Too many SKUs and higher waste |
| Labour | Roster to forecast by half-hour, train adjacent stations, time studies and award compliance | Higher sales per paid labour-hour | Excess shifts or unsafe understaffing |
| Distribution | Route density and cut-off design, vehicle utilisation, failed-drop monitoring | Lower cost per delivered item | Remote sites erode margin |
| Utilities | Refrigeration audit, preventive maintenance, shift-off checks, negotiated energy arrangements | Lower downtime and energy waste | Spoilage and emergency repairs |
| Rent/site | Benchmark effective rent incl outgoings, annual rises, permitted use and options | Avoids high fixed-cost sites | Cheap acquisition with expensive lease |
| Marketing | CAC/payback by campaign, test/holdout, stop-loss budgets | Spend on profitable customers | Vanity reach and unprofitable discounts |
| Technology | Automate capture, quote, batch planning, dispatch and reconciliation | Fewer manual errors and faster response | Duplicated admin across stores |

**Measurement plan:** establish 30-day baseline and weekly scorecard for COGS per accepted finished unit, cooked yield, minutes per 100 minis, waste/returns, labour cost per order, power/unit, cost/drop, average drive time, order-to-delivery error, contribution by SKU and by site. Set improvement targets only after baseline rather than asserting a 10% labour saving as fact.

**Commercial negotiation checks:** supplier rebates tied to volume; repayment/credit term impact; price escalation clauses; backup sources; liability and product recall; equipment service histories, repair and replacement capex; market comparison of related-party procurement; avoid exclusivity unless a quantifiable benefit compensates for concentration risk.
