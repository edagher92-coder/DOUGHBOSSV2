# Brand identity

Dough Boss is a contemporary Lebanese bakery with three shops in Sydney (Revesby, Bankstown, Roselands). This section sets out the identity the catering box carries, and every item that follows it, so the brand looks the same on a box, a shirt, a shop window and the website.

**Status:** CONCEPT FOR DEVELOPMENT (3 October 2026). The wordmark is the one on the live site today. The trade mark position is an open question (see the decisions register). Nothing here is to be printed in volume or published until the gates in the status section are cleared.

## The wordmark

The logo is the live site's wordmark: **DOUGH BOSS.** in Bebas Neue capitals, letter-spaced 0.13 em, inside a single-rule box, with the full stop in ember red. It is rebuilt exactly from the site's CSS (22 px type, padding 8 / 8 / 7 / 11 px, 2 px rule), so every application scales those ratios.

- **Construction:** box rule = 2/22 of the type size; padding top 8/22, right 8/22, bottom 7/22, left 11/22; cap height about 0.70 of the type size, centred in the line box.
- **Colourways:** cream on black (primary); char on kraft or paper (mono, for one-colour jobs; the full stop goes char too); cream on ember is NOT allowed.
- **Clear space:** at least the height of the "D" all round the box.
- **Minimum size:** box width 25 mm for digital print, 45 mm for screen print and DTF, 90 mm for embroidery, 110 mm for cut vinyl (the 1.45 % rule weight sets the limit), carton flexo: Bebas 11 mm type or larger so the rule stays at least 1.0 mm.
- **Don't:** stretch, outline, add effects, re-letter, change the spacing, drop the box on its own, or let a generator draw it. In photographs it is always composited from the vector artwork.

## Voice

Short, warm, confident, plain. Speak about the food and the occasion, never the customer's background. No hype, no false urgency, no superlatives we cannot prove. One idea per line. Australian English.

## Photography direction

Dark, real, high contrast, personal, professional but never perfect. One soft key light from the left, deep shadows to the lower right, true blacks. Real surfaces (brushed steel, dark timber, greaseproof paper), real crumbs, grease halos on the liner, a slightly crooked seal, handwriting on the label, flour on the bench. No people, no hands, no steam or haze, no lemons, no invented shop settings. Logos and print are never drawn by an image generator: plates are made blank and the vector artwork is composited as print. Real photographs of the real box and the real bakes replace every concept image before publication.
